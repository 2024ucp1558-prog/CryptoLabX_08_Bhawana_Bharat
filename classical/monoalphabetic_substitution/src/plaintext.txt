Many years earlier, the analysis of the one time pad had to await the groundbreaking work of Shannon, who introduced the notion of perfect secrecy.

In this chapter we studied perfectly secret encryption. Some other cryptographic problems can also be solved with perfect security. A notable example is the problem of message authentication where the aim is to prevent an adversary from undetectably modifying a message sent from one party to another.

We study this problem in depth in cryptography. Perfectly secure systems provide strong protection because an attacker cannot learn useful information about the message from the ciphertext alone.

Perfect secrecy is an important concept in modern cryptography. The one time pad is a well known example of an encryption scheme that can achieve perfect secrecy when the key is chosen correctly and used only once.
